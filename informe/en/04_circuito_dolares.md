# 4. The dollar circuit

This chapter follows dollars from the moment they enter the country until they leave it or stay in the reserves. All figures are in millions of dollars. The main source is the FX balance (balance cambiario) of the Banco Central de la República Argentina (BCRA, the central bank), which records every purchase and sale of foreign currency in the official FX market month by month, by concept and by sector, on a cash basis. The window runs from December 2023 to August 2026.

::: {.destacado custom-style="Destacado"}
**The dollars that enter the country do not automatically become available to pay public debt.**

Goods exports brought in USD 224,892 million during the term that began in December 2023. Imports (160,322), services, interest and dollar purchases by households and firms all compete for them. Those purchases totaled USD 48,476 million net, 2.3 times the increase in reserves through the FX market.

*Scope.* Net purchases of banknotes and foreign currency are an operation in the official FX market. They are neither a physical outflow from the country nor a permanent loss of reserves: part of those dollars stays deposited in local banks and can return to the reserves as reserve requirements, or to the market as sales.
:::

Three features organize the rest of the chapter.

**Exporters supply most of the dollars.** Goods exports brought in USD 224,892 million, 46% of everything that came in. Against goods imports (160,322) they left a surplus of USD 64,570 million. Services subtracted 21,795.

**Lenders move almost as much as they deliver.** International organizations other than the IMF disbursed USD 58,064 million and collected 51,797 in principal and 5,839 in interest, so in net terms they contributed about USD 400 million in almost three years: they refinanced what was falling due. With the IMF the net was positive, USD 7,286 million (20,957 in disbursements, minus 5,437 in principal and 8,234 in interest).

**The largest outflow after imports is dollar hoarding.** Households and firms bought USD 70,484 million in banknotes and foreign currency and sold 22,008. The net, USD 48,476 million, is 2.3 times the increase in reserves over the whole period (20,834).

It helps to be precise about what this series measures. It is the line "compra-venta de billetes y divisas sin fines específicos" (purchase and sale of banknotes and foreign currency with no specific purpose) of the resident non-financial private sector in the FX balance: what households and firms bought and sold in the official market, on a cash basis. It does not say where those dollars went afterwards. They may stay in a safe-deposit box, leave the country or be deposited in a dollar account at a local bank; in the last case the bank can lend them or hold them as reserve requirements at the BCRA, where they add to gross reserves again. Nor does it include purchases through the securities market (MEP and contado con liquidación) or card spending abroad, which the balance records under services. The report calls it private dollar hoarding because that is the use the series assumes, and treats it as an outflow from the FX market, without assuming that those dollars were lost to the Argentine economy.

## 4.2 Before and after the end of the cepo

Purchases of foreign currency by individuals were freed on 14 April 2025, when the cepo (capital controls) was lifted for them. The break changes the circuit completely.

| Jan to Mar 2025 | 17,579 | 16,741 | 438 | 969 | −5,628 | 0 |

*Source: BCRA FX balance (SRC-1604). USD million.*

Until March 2025, households sold more dollars than they bought. From April, 97% of all gross purchases in the period (68,452 of USD 70,484 million) took place in 17 months. Monthly purchases went from between 119 and 173 million in the first quarter of 2025 to 2,513 in April, peaked in September (7,759, before the elections) and settled at between 2,840 and 4,095 a month in 2026.

What came in from the IMF under the new agreement, about USD 15.5 billion since April 2025, covers somewhat less than a quarter of what households bought over the same span. Seen from the dollar circuit, the IMF agreement and private dollar hoarding were two sides of the same phase: the Fund advanced dollars and, over the same span, individuals bought a much larger amount in the official market. The report records the coincidence in the numbers; the causal link between the two is beyond what these data can prove.

## 4.3 The IMF and the other organizations

The Extended Fund Facility (EFF) agreement of April 2025 is for SDR 15,267 million over 48 months (SRC-1611). By 30 September 2026, SDR 11,452 million had been drawn:

- SDR 9,160 million, about USD 12 billion, on 15 April 2025;
- SDR 1,529 million, about USD 2 billion, on 4 August 2025, with the first review;
- SDR 763 million, about USD 1 billion, on 26 May 2026, with the second review, which had been postponed since November 2025.

The third review, with SDR 636 million available since 31 July 2026, was still unapproved on 16 October. Five tranches of SDR 635 or 636 million remain until January 2029.

The USD 12 billion of April 2025 did not stay in the Treasury. On 6 May 2025 the Treasury used it to buy back from the BCRA Letras Intransferibles (non-transferable Treasury notes held by the BCRA) with a face value of USD 13,617 million (RC 21/2025, SRC-1257). The Treasury's dollar deposits at the BCRA went from USD 15,448 million at the end of April to 3,068 at the end of May. The dollars stayed in the reserves, and the BCRA exchanged a dollar asset against the Treasury (the Letras) for liquid dollars. Chapter 5 returns to these buybacks.

## 4.4 External support

Between 2025 and 2026 the public sector paid its dollar debt, sustained an exchange-rate band regime and allowed households and firms to buy dollars without limit, all at the same time. This section asks which public commitments and which external support made that possible. It separates what actually happened from what was announced, from the conditions for access to each form of support and from the effects someone infers.

*Positive sign: inflow to the BCRA or the Treasury. Sources gathered on 18 October 2026. Originals with an SRC code are stored with their hash; two BCRA press releases were read on the web because the portal returned an error when saving them, and press sources are cited with their outlet. As of 18 October, no primary source documents a staff-level agreement on the third IMF review.*

**The IMF.** It is the only support in the table that left dollars in a large amount and for a long term: it disbursed about USD 15 billion between April 2025 and May 2026, which under an Extended Fund Facility are repaid between four and a half and ten years after each drawing. Over the same span the Treasury kept paying principal and interest on the previous program, which is why the IMF's net contribution over the whole term was USD 7,286 million (section 4.3). Its conditions refer to reserve accumulation and exchange-rate flexibility. The reserve targets were missed in June and in December 2025, and the IMF approved both reviews anyway, after the government adopted corrective measures and with the following targets lowered (section 4.5). The IMF press releases do not set the exchange-rate band: the band and its adjustment for inflation since January 2026 are BCRA decisions.

**The US Treasury.** The support of October 2025 came from the US Treasury, through its Exchange Stabilization Fund (ESF), and not from the Federal Reserve: no source documents a Federal Reserve loan or swap with the BCRA. The signed agreement is for up to USD 20 billion. What the ESF financial statements show is a drawing of USD 2,500 million in October 2025, repaid in full in December, and no outstanding balance through August 2026. The US Treasury also bought pesos in the market in October 2025, but no primary source publishes the amount. Access carried an explicit political condition: on 14 October 2025 the US president said the support depended on the government's electoral result. A private bank facility for another USD 20 billion, announced the same month, did not materialize, according to the press. As of 30 June 2026 the USD 20 billion line remained in force with no balance drawn (SRC-9028). In those months the ESF also sold Argentina special drawing rights for USD 872 million in October 2025, 808 in January 2026 and 819 in April 2026, to pay the IMF (SRC-9013, SRC-9015 and SRC-9026): these were purchases paid with Argentine dollars, with no financing.

**How much the US Treasury earned.** The US Treasury said the operation left it a profit. The letter from its office of legislative affairs to two senators, dated 20 January 2026, speaks of "tens of millions in profit for Americans" (SRC-9030), and the Minister of Economy said on a streaming program in early October 2026 that the United States "no puso nada, ganó plata" (put in nothing and made money) (Infobae, 5 October 2026). Neither statement gives a figure. US official sources allow it to be bounded, though not calculated exactly.

*The ESF monthly statements value the pesos in dollars at each month-end exchange rate. Table 1 of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York has rows for the euro and the yen, and none for the peso because the ESF held no pesos at either the start or the end of the quarter, but its totals include what happened to the pesos in between.*

The ESF's pesos came from two operations. One was the drawing on the agreement: the BCRA delivered pesos in exchange for USD 2,500 million and bought them back in December. The other was market purchases, spot and through the contado con liquidación, which the Federal Reserve Bank of New York report confirms without giving amounts, dates or exchange rates (SRC-9024). The press estimated them at about USD 2,000 to 2,500 million, at an average exchange rate close to 1,470 pesos (the consultancy 1816, cited by El Ágora on 30 October 2025; medium confidence).

Subtracting the euro and yen rows from the totals in table 1 leaves what corresponds to the pesos: about USD 119 million in earnings, about 116 million in revaluation losses and −2 in net purchases. The calculation is our own, by difference, and each figure has a rounding margin of one or two million. The 119 million match the jump in October interest in the monthly statements, which went from about 4 million in a month without pesos to 125.6.

With these data the profit lies between two bounds. If the revaluation loss was an actual loss, the pesos left the ESF a result close to zero. But in the swap portion the BCRA had to buy back the pesos at the agreed exchange rate, and the table shows only the peso asset, without that counterpart; to the extent that the swap offset the revaluation, the profit approaches the 120 million in earnings. The Treasury's "tens of millions" fit within that range. The official figure also does not say whether it deducts what those dollars would have earned in US Treasury securities, where the ESF normally keeps them invested: by our own calculation, about USD 20 million over those two and a half months. The report cannot go further with what has been published. Nor do the documents say where the pesos were invested or who paid those earnings. The press reported that the pesos bought in the market were placed in an unannounced BCRA bill that was unwound before the end of October (Infobae, 5 November 2025; medium confidence). If so, part of the US Treasury's profit was paid by the BCRA, in pesos, and forms part of the peso cost of the circuit that chapter 3 describes.

**What the US Treasury has to report.** US law requires the Treasury to send the Banking committees of both chambers, within 30 days after each month, an ESF statement with all agreements and all operations of the month (31 U.S.C. §5302(c)). Those statements were published, and the figures in this section come from them, but they report balances and aggregate results, without the detail of each purchase. A loan to a foreign government for more than six months within a year also requires a written statement from the president to Congress (§5302(b)); the October drawing was repaid in two months and stayed below that threshold. On 17 December 2025 two senators asked by letter for the detail of the peso purchases and the text of the agreement, which the Treasury treated as classified information, and noted that it had not been delivered to the Foreign Relations Committee as the Case Act requires for international agreements (SRC-9029). The Treasury's reply of 20 January 2026 did not include that detail (SRC-9030). The annual audit of the ESF, carried out by the firm KPMG under the supervision of the Treasury inspector general, covers the fiscal year ended 30 September 2025 and mentions the swap as a subsequent event, without the market purchases (SRC-9027). The audit for fiscal year 2026, the first that will include these operations in its statements, should be published around January 2027 if the calendar repeats.

**The banks and China.** The BCRA's repos with international banks total USD 6 billion outstanding, secured with Treasury bonds, and in July 2026 they were renewed to 2028. The swap with the People's Bank of China was renewed, and the activated tranche did not have to be repaid during the term; how much of that tranche is still in use is a figure the press reports inconsistently.

**What can be stated.** There is no documentation of a formal exchange-rate guarantee from anyone. What the data show is support that expanded the intervention capacity of the BCRA and the Treasury and that probably influenced expectations: actual use of the agreement with the United States was small relative to its size and lasted two months. The claim that the program would have collapsed without that support is a counterfactual interpretation. The US Treasury Secretary, among others, made it, and no accounting identity in the report demonstrates it. What the accounts do show is the order of magnitude: IMF drawings since April 2025 and outstanding repos add up to about USD 21 billion, less than half of the USD 48,476 million in net private dollar purchases during the term.

## 4.5 Reserves

The report uses the definition of net international reserves in the IMF agreement: gross reserves minus the BCRA's short-term foreign-currency liabilities, without subtracting Treasury deposits. Under that definition the program targets are floors for the cumulative change in net reserves, and not for their level.

|---|---:|---:|---|
| 13-06-2025 | +0.2 | −3.3 | IMF, second review (SRC-1263), table 2 |
| End-2025 | −1.6 | −12.1 | same |
| End-March 2026 (indicative) | −1.6 | −10.5 | same |
| End-June 2026 | −8.6 (previously +3.2) | −6.7 (own estimate; range −9.7 to −4.7) | same; own estimate with SRC-1625 |
| End-2026 | −4.1 | no data | same |

*USD billion, cumulative change since the start of the program.*

The 2025 targets were not met, and the end-2025 target was missed by about USD 10 billion. Instead of declaring non-compliance, each review lowered the next target: the June 2026 target was +3,200 million and the second review took it to −8,600.

**June 2026, no official figure.** The IMF will publish the June outcome with the third review, which as of 17 October had no staff-level agreement. The report estimates it with the BCRA reserves spreadsheet (SRC-1625) and the program definition: to the change in gross reserves over the half-year (+3,775 million) it adds the fall in foreign-currency reserve requirements, which are a liability (+2,713), and subtracts the IMF disbursement of May (−1,043), which the program does not count. Accumulation in the first half comes to about +5,400 million, and the change since end-2024 to about −6,700: above the lowered floor of −8,600. The level of net reserves at end-June stands at about −6,400 million. The estimate depends on two assumptions. If the USD 3,000 million repo with banks from January 2026, at 372 days, were counted as a liability, the change would be −9,700 and the target would have been missed. If the half-year payments on the Bonos para la Reconstrucción de una Argentina Libre (BOPREAL, BCRA dollar bonds) were treated as repayment of a short-term liability, it would be −4,700. Two private estimates point in the same direction (Equilibra, +6,300 in the half-year; Centro de Economía Política Argentina or CEPA, −3,100 since end-2024 with the other 2025 base). The level of net reserves at end-2025 was −11,800 million according to the same report, and the IMF projects −1,800 for end-2026 (in July 2025 it projected +8,400).

Two figures coexist without contradicting each other, although they are often presented as if they did. The BCRA's gross reserves were USD 49,323 million on 8 October 2026 (BCRA API). The IMF table gives "gross reserves" of USD 27,600 million for end-2025, with the program measure, which leaves out items the BCRA does count. The two answer different questions, and it is advisable to cite each by its name. In addition, the same IMF document gives a change of −12,100 for 2025 in the staff report and −9,600 in the letter of intent, without explaining the difference (chapter 7).

## 4.6 The dollars from export duties

Export duties are assessed in dollars on the FOB value (free on board: the value of the goods loaded on the ship) and paid in pesos. Between December 2023 and August 2026 the Treasury collected ARS 28.6 trillion at August 2026 prices from them. Measured in dollars, they came to about USD 6,600 million in 2024, 6,000 in 2025 and 4,600 from January to September 2026.

Those dollars never passed through the Treasury. The exporter sells the foreign currency in the market, the buyer (the BCRA or a private party) pays in pesos, and with those pesos the exporter pays the duty. The dollar circuit records the whole export; the peso circuit, the duty. Annex A studies what would change if part of those dollars went directly to the Treasury.

## 4.7 What remains to be paid

The phase 2 contractual calendar, built instrument by instrument with the SIGADE cut-off at 30 June 2026, gives a firm gross need of USD 22,145 million between July 2026 and June 2027. Net of payments to holders within the public sector itself, the consolidated need is USD 18,775 million. Two months account for almost half: July 2026 (5,079) and January 2027 (5,067). For the breakdown by creditor and the profile of the following years, see chapter 2.

On the other side, the Treasury's dollar deposits at the BCRA were USD 1,230 million on 14 October 2026, against 2,374 million in maturities between October and December and 5,067 in January. The gap is covered with dollar purchases paid in pesos, with new dollar debt or with disbursements from international organizations. That is the subject of the next chapter.
