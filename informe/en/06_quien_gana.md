# 6. Who receives the flows and who takes on the obligations

::: {.destacado custom-style="Destacado"}
**Private dollarization and public obligations should be read together.**

While individuals and companies made net purchases of USD 48,476 million in the official market, the IMF disbursed USD 20,957 million and the public sector's foreign-currency debt to external creditors rose by 14,353 million. Some acquired dollar assets; the public sector took on the dollar liabilities that will have to be paid later.

*Scope.* The chapter describes who received the flows and who ended up with the obligations. It does not measure the final gains or losses of each group.
:::

The previous chapters measured where pesos and dollars flowed. This one asks whose hands they ended up in. The project's original question was who wins and who loses, and the data allow part of it to be answered. Talking about gains would require distinguishing four things: the financial income someone collects (interest, a premium), the real return on that income after inflation and the exchange rate, the gain realized when the asset is sold or matures, and the change in risk exposure (who ends up with an asset or a liability in dollars, or linked to inflation). Official sources say a fair amount about who pays and little about who collects (chapter 7), and almost nothing about each group's final result. For that reason the chapter describes the flows and the obligations, states in each case which of the four things it measures, and leaves the assessment to the reader.

## 6.1 In the peso circuit

### Who paid the interest

The central change of the term that began in December 2023 is one of payer. Until July 2024, the cost of remunerating pesos was paid by the Banco Central de la República Argentina (BCRA, the central bank). Since then the Treasury pays it.

| Financial year | Interest paid by the BCRA | Of which LELIQ and pases | Interest the BCRA collected on Treasury securities | BCRA result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 28.0 | 27.7 | 7.5 | −4.3 |
| 2024 | 17.1 | 16.1 | 29.5 | +19.4 |
| 2025 | 1.6 | 0.3 | 23.3 | +34.3 |

*Trillion pesos of each year-end. Source: BCRA financial statements (SRC-1673 and following).*

In 2023 the BCRA paid banks 28 trillion pesos on LELIQ (BCRA liquidity bills) and pases (BCRA repos absorbing pesos), and was losing money. In 2025 it paid almost no interest and collected 23.3 trillion on the Treasury securities in its portfolio. With that profit it transferred profits to the Treasury: ARS 42.9 trillion at August 2026 prices during the term (April 2025 and May 2026). To a large extent, the profits the Treasury records as a source of financing are interest the Treasury itself had previously paid to the BCRA.

On the Treasury's side, peso interest was hardly paid in cash: ARS 2.2 trillion over the whole term. It was capitalized. Accrued unpaid interest on LECAP, BONCAP and other capitalizing instruments adds up to about 66 trillion current pesos between the first quarter of 2024 and the second quarter of 2026 (chapter 3). That interest does not appear in the published fiscal balance, but it is added to principal and paid at maturity with new debt or with the surplus.

### Who collected

**Banks.** In 2023 and 2024 they were the main recipients of the interest paid on absorption. The table measures financial income and accounting results, in constant pesos; it does not measure how much of that income was a real gain for banks after paying their depositors and after inflation.

| Year | Financial margin | Result on securities | Premiums on pases | Total result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 101.9 | 93.6 | 30.2 | 14.9 |
| 2024 | 89.1 | 34.5 | 25.2 | 15.9 |
| 2025 | 50.2 | 22.8 | 0.0 | 2.6 |
| 2026, January to August | 37.4 | 14.2 | 0.3 | 3.6 |

*Trillion pesos at August 2026 prices. Source: BCRA, Informe sobre Bancos (report on banks).*

In 2023, the result on securities and premiums on pases equalled 122% of banks' financial margin. In 2026 they equal 39%. Banks passed part of that income on to their depositors: they paid 109.6 trillion in interest in 2023. LEFI, between July 2024 and July 2025, left them about ARS 3.4 trillion in current pesos of interest, paid by the Treasury.

**Mutual funds.** National debt held by open-ended mutual funds went from ARS 5.4 trillion in current pesos in December 2023 to ARS 21.4 trillion in August 2026 (Cámara Argentina de Fondos Comunes de Inversión, CAFCI, the mutual fund industry chamber). Fixed-income funds hold almost all of it: in a sample from the Comisión Nacional de Valores (CNV, the securities regulator), at August 2026 they held 37% in CER bonds and 32% in LECAP. Money market funds, with about ARS 63 trillion in assets, hold almost no LECAP: they place money in time deposits and cauciones (exchange-traded secured loans), so their link to public debt runs through the banks.

**The public sector itself.** 60% of peso debt is held by the BCRA, the FGS (the pension system's reserve fund) and Banco Nación (chapter 2). For that 60%, capitalized interest is a transfer within the State. At June 2026 the FGS held CER bonds for ARS 54.7 trillion (44% of its portfolio) and between 10.6% and 16.3% of LECAP and BONCAP.

**What is not known.** There is no official figure for how much peso debt each private sector holds, nor by instrument. Private estimates (1816 in May 2026: 49% BCRA and FGS, 28% banks, 23% funds, insurers, non-residents and others) are consistent with official data, but they do not replace a series.

## 6.2 In the dollar circuit

**Those who hoarded.** Net purchases of dollars by individuals and companies (private dollar hoarding) were USD 48,476 million during the term, and USD 68,452 million of gross purchases took place after April 2025. This is the largest outflow from the FX market after imports. Whoever bought dollars changed their exposure: they went from holding a peso asset to holding a dollar asset, the currency in which the State needs to pay its debt. Whether that gave them a gain or a loss depends on the peso rate they gave up and the exchange rate at which they bought, and the report does not measure it.

**Private creditors.** The National Government paid USD 10,456 million in interest through the FX market, mostly to holders of Globales and Bonares, and paid all principal that fell due. Private dollar debt was not restructured during the period.

**The IMF and other multilaterals.** The IMF disbursed USD 20,957 million and collected 5,437 million in principal and 8,234 million in interest: it contributed 7,286 million net and became the Treasury's main dollar creditor (USD 57,706 million). The other international organizations refinanced almost exactly what they collected.

**Reserves.** They rose by USD 20,834 million through the FX market, less than half of what went out in net dollar hoarding. Net reserves, under the program definition, remained negative.

## 6.3 Between the Treasury and the BCRA

A single operation between the Treasury and the BCRA does not change the public sector's debt to third parties, but it does change who bears what. When it is paid with a new loan, as with the 2025 buybacks funded by the IMF disbursement, the consolidated total does change (chapter 5). There were six during the term.

| Operation | What the BCRA delivered | What it received | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AL35, February 2024 | AL35 bonds, original face value (VNO) USD 7,596 million | ARS 2.951 trillion (about USD 3,534 million) | 46% of face value | RC 13/2024, SRC-2064 |
| TDE25, January 2025 | BONAR DUAL for about USD 4,162 million | LECAP, no cash payment | | RC 5/2025, SRC-2063 |
| Letras Intransferibles, May 2025 | VNO 13,617 | USD 12,000 million from the IMF | 88% average | RC 21/2025, SRC-1257 |
| Letras Intransferibles, August 2025 | VNO 3,269 | USD 2,000 million from the IMF | 61% | RC 37/2025, SRC-2042 |
| Letras Intransferibles, May 2026 | VNO 20,434 | ARS 18.4 trillion of its own profits | 65% average | RC 27/2026, SRC-2031 |
| Letras Intransferibles, August 2026 | VNO 1,303 | USD 781.5 million that the BCRA itself paid for BOPREAL | 60% | RC 47/2026, SRC-2043 (press) |

In all of them, the BCRA delivered dollar claims on the Treasury and received in exchange dollars, pesos or peso securities worth less than face value. For the Treasury, each operation reduced nominal dollar debt at a lower price. For the BCRA, the counterpart depended on the route: with IMF dollars, it swapped an illiquid asset for reserves; with pesos, it swapped a dollar asset for pesos it had itself created when transferring profits. Consolidating both balance sheets, the public sector swapped dollar debt to the BCRA for dollar debt to the IMF (in 2025) and for pesos (in 2024 and 2026).

## 6.4 What can be said

- In pesos, the cost of absorbing liquidity was paid by the BCRA until mid-2024 and collected mainly by banks. Since then the Treasury pays it, mostly as capitalized interest that does not appear in the fiscal balance, and it is collected by banks, mutual funds and, for 60%, the public sector itself.
- In dollars, the main destination of available dollars was private dollar hoarding, and the main net provider to the public sector was the IMF.
- Between the Treasury and the BCRA, the BCRA gave up dollar claims on the Treasury below face value, in exchange for IMF reserves or pesos.
