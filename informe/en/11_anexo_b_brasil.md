# Annex B. Brazil

Brazil serves as a limited contrast. It is a large economy in the region, a commodity exporter like Argentina, in which the two circuits barely cross at the Treasury.

**The debt is almost entirely in its own currency.** According to a classification derived from the securities' codes, which is not official, in July 2026 federal public debt was split as follows: 46.4% linked to the Selic rate, 27.2% indexed to the broad consumer price index (IPCA), 23.5% at a fixed rate and 2.7% linked to the exchange rate. In September 2017 the exchange-rate share was 2.4%. The Brazilian Treasury does not need to buy dollars to pay almost any of its debt.

**The exporter decides where to hold the foreign currency.** Since Lei 14.286/2021, FX operations are free, are carried out with institutions authorized by the Banco Central do Brasil (BCB, Brazil's central bank) at a freely agreed rate, and keeping export proceeds abroad is optional (SRC-0505). The obligation to convert them was relaxed in 2006 and in 2021.

**There are no export duties, with exceptions.** The only one in force in 2026 is a temporary 12% duty on crude oil, from July 2026, extended in September for 60 days (Resoluções Gecex 938/2026 and 957/2026).

**Reserves are seven times Argentina's.** On 13 October 2026 the Banco Central do Brasil held USD 367,337 million in reserves, against BCRA gross reserves of USD 49,323 million on 8 October and negative net reserves under the IMF program.

The contrast shows the report's central argument from the other side. When public debt is in the currency the state issues, the dollar circuit stays in the hands of exporters, importers and investors, and the Treasury barely takes part. When a large part of the debt is in dollars, as in Argentina, the Treasury depends on the dollar circuit to pay, and the peso surplus has to be converted into dollars through one of the channels in chapter 5.

Two limitations: the classification of Brazilian debt by indexer is our own and was not checked against the official report of the Tesouro Nacional, and the BCB's general government gross debt series have a methodological break that was not resolved.
