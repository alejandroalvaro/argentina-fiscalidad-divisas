# 7. Opacity as data

Reconstructing the two circuits took more work than it should have. This chapter treats that effort as a result: what had to be gathered, which series do not exist, which figures change from one official publication to another and which errors turned up in official files. None of these problems, on its own, prevents us from knowing how much the State owes. Together they make it very hard to know in which currency, to whom and with what resources it is paid.

::: {.destacado custom-style="Destacado"}
**The debt stock is transparent; reconstructing its movements requires cross-checking publications that use different criteria.**

Between December 2023 and August 2026, the savings-investment-financing (AIF) table of the Secretaría de Hacienda (Budget Secretariat) records USD 121,405 million of "foreign-currency" debt amortization. Of that figure, 45,325 million was debt denominated in dollars that was paid in pesos. Reading all of that amortization as actual payments in foreign currency would overstate those payments, which were 76,080 million, by about 60%.

*Scope.* The percentage applies to that source's amortization in that window, with actual foreign-currency payments as the denominator; it does not extend to the debt stock. It follows from classifying by currency of denomination, a criterion the statistics apply consistently, and it implies no intention to conceal.
:::

## 7.1 The size of the work

To arrive at the diagrams and tables of this report we had to gather:

- 1,291 distinct sources, in 50 search batches, with 1,404 recorded claims;
- 1,214 original documents stored, each identified by its SHA-256 hash, and 1,540 recorded downloads;
- more than 750 debt operations in SIGADE and the issuance terms of 101 instruments, loaded by hand from prospectuses and resolutions;
- about 640 questions that remained unanswered in each batch's log of items not found.

The debt stock is transparent: SIGADE, the quarterly workbook and the maturity profile match to the dollar, a virtue not every country has. The opacity lies in the movements, in the currency in which debt is paid and in who holds each bond.

## 7.2 Series that do not exist

- **Treasury dollar deposits**, in dollars. The BCRA publishes the Government's foreign-currency deposits converted into pesos. To know how many dollars the Treasury has, they must be converted back day by day.
- **Treasury dollar deposits at Banco Nación.** There is no series.
- **Treasury FX purchases** broken down by channel (from the BCRA, in the market, in block operations). The BCRA reports purchases from the BCRA in pesos, and market purchases appear in press releases with figures that later change.
- **Which pesos buy each dollar.** There is no release, purchase by purchase, saying whether the pesos came from the surplus, from debt or from deposits.
- **The savings-investment-financing table broken down by instrument or by creditor.** Debt amortization and placement are published as totals by currency, without saying what was paid.
- **A methodological note** on how debt denominated in dollars and paid in pesos is classified (dólar linked, BONTE and Letras del Tesoro (LETES) that pay in pesos, BONAR DUAL). The 2026 Classification Manual does not name those instruments and contains two written rules that contradict each other.
- **Peso debt in pesos.** The Secretaría de Finanzas (Finance Secretariat) publishes peso debt converted into dollars.
- **Holdings of each bond.** Who holds each instrument is not published, either inside or outside the public sector. The FGS (the ANSES pension reserve fund) publishes its portfolio by category and never by individual security.
- **The LEFI.** There is no series of the total issued or of the part that stayed at the BCRA. The capitalization formula is published as an image in the Boletín Oficial (official gazette).
- **The interest the BCRA pays, by month.** The financial statements are annual and are restated.
- **The average rate on the stock of time deposits** and the official ranking of mutual funds by assets.

None of these differences is an error in itself. Many have an explanation: face value against cash value, trade date against settlement date, different criteria. The problem is that the explanation is almost never published, and the reader has to reconstruct it from the original documents.

## 7.4 What is called "foreign currency"

The most important case for this report is the classification of debt by currency in the savings-investment-financing table of the Secretaría de Hacienda. Between December 2023 and August 2026, that table's "foreign-currency" debt amortization adds up to USD 121,405 million. Of that figure, 45,325 million was debt denominated in dollars that was paid in pesos, so the amount paid in dollars was 76,080 million. The same happens on the placement side: of 83,563 million "in foreign currency", 19,413 was placed for pesos and 64,150 came in as dollars.

The criterion also changes between publications:

- the 2024 Cuenta de Inversión (the annual budget execution report) counts the dólar linked BONTE as a foreign-currency placement, and the 2025 one does not;
- the Oficina Nacional de Crédito Público (National Public Credit Office) classified, until 2025, dollar BONTE that pay in pesos as foreign currency and dollar-linked bills (LELINK) as domestic currency; in 2026 it switched to classifying by currency of payment and moved the BONTE to domestic currency;
- the Finance Secretariat bulletin counts dólar linked debt as local currency in table A.3 and as foreign currency in table A.1;
- table A.4 of the bulletin includes cashless exchanges: RC 16/2024 retired dollar BONAR DUAL and BONTE for 25,173 million that the savings-investment-financing table does not show.

A reader who wants to know how many dollars the Treasury needed and reads all "foreign-currency" amortization as payments in foreign currency overstates them by about 60% (121,405 against 76,080 million, between December 2023 and August 2026). Classification by currency of denomination is a legitimate statistical criterion; the problem is that the classification by currency of payment is not published next to it. To correct this, the report had to reconstruct the debt paid in pesos operation by operation from auctions and resolutions.

## 7.5 Errors in official files and unexplained operations

- **The financial structure published by the Oficina Nacional de Crédito Público** repeats the amortization of the Global 2030 in the Global 2035.
- **The official maturity profile** understates twelve-month principal by USD 32.9 billion, mainly because it does not include what was issued after the cutoff (chapter 2).
- **The FODER guarantee bills** appear in SIGADE as ordinary debt, with a mechanical schedule of 266 million per year, when they are contingent debt.
- **Three peso bills** (TMF27, TMG27 and TZX27) show balance reductions with no published operation that explains them.
- **RC 60/2026** never published its amounts.
- **RC 30/2026** authorized a payment of USD 1,040 million that does not appear in the Treasury's deposits.
- **The export files of the Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero (ARCA, the tax and customs agency)** contain 81 records with impossible prices. For example, in February 2021 soybeans (1201.90.00) appear with USD 6,508 million FOB for 37,325 tonnes, 174 dollars per kilo against a median of 0.41. The report sets them aside with a unit-value filter and lists them.

## 7.6 Delays and portals

- **SIGADE** as of 30 June 2026 was published on 18 September, about 80 days later. In that interval the fortnightly auctions change about USD 36 billion of twelve-month principal. The 30 September cutoff had not been published as of 17 October.
- **ANSES** blocks automated download of the FGS reports; several 2023 and 2024 reports return a 404 error.
- **The IMF** blocks automated download of its reports; the copies published by the BCRA were used.
- **The BCRA** returned repeated 502 errors for the August 2026 Informe sobre Bancos (banking report), and some file paths change without notice.
- **The mutual fund chamber (CAFCI)** and the Ministry of Agriculture data portal reject automated download.
- **The datos.gob.ar series interface** drops observations at random. The static files have to be used.

## 7.7 Our own errors

The method also detected errors by the agents that did this work, and they are worth reporting because they show how easy it is to go wrong with these sources:

- a table of monetary base factors prepared in one batch was wrong in 27 of 45 months, because it added the workbook's annual block to January;
- an interest figure that was in billions of pesos was read as dollars;
- two buybacks of Letras Intransferibles were assumed to have been paid with pesos, when they were paid with IMF dollars and with dollars the BCRA delivered for BOPREAL;
- it was inferred that dollar debt placed for pesos was recorded as domestic currency, and the auction data showed the opposite;
- in the second draft, the total of Treasury dollar purchases came from the BCRA monthly report and its breakdown by channel from the daily series. The two do not add up to the same amount, because the monthly report omits a purchase of 787 million from the BCRA on 5 February 2026. The third draft uses a single series, the monthly report, with that purchase added (chapter 5);
- in the same draft, USD 16,703 million appeared as the face value of the debt to the BCRA canceled with pesos. It is the dollar equivalent of what was paid; the face value was 28,030 million.

Each error was corrected against the original document. As the corrections came in, the Treasury's unexplained dollar difference fell from −18,221 to −4,150 and then to −3,522 million, and rose to −4,309 when the February 2026 purchase was added: that purchase closes the month in which it occurred, and the sum over all months, which offsets errors of opposite sign, grows.

## 7.8 What would be needed

The list follows directly from the above. It would be enough for the Secretaría de Finanzas to publish peso debt in pesos and holdings by sector and instrument; for the Budget Secretariat to break down amortization and placement by instrument and by currency of payment, with a methodological note; for the BCRA to publish Treasury dollar deposits in dollars and Treasury purchases by channel; and for operations between the Treasury and the BCRA to be reported with their counterpart and their price. Almost all of this already exists inside the administration, because without those data the records that are published could not be produced.
