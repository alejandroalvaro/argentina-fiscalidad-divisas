# Glossary

Every acronym, instrument or concept that appears in the text is here, in the alphabetical order of the original Spanish terms. In parentheses, the currency of the circuit it belongs to, where relevant.

**A 3500.** Reference exchange rate the BCRA publishes every business day (Comunicación A 3500). The report uses it to convert pesos into dollars, and the reverse, when a comparison requires it.

**Financial assets (activos financieros).** In the savings-investment-financing table, what the public sector does with pesos that is neither spending nor paying debt: depositing them at the BCRA or at Banco Nación, buying securities, lending. An "increase" means placing pesos in those assets; a "use" means withdrawing them. (Pesos.)

**Adelantos transitorios.** Temporary advances: peso loans from the BCRA to the Treasury, within limits set in the BCRA Charter (Carta Orgánica). They create monetary base. (Pesos.)

**Savings-investment-financing (AIF).** The table (ahorro-inversión-financiamiento) in which the Budget Secretariat (Secretaría de Hacienda) publishes the accounts of the national public sector on a cash basis: revenue, spending, balance, and financial sources and applications. It classifies debt by its currency of denomination, so it calls "foreign currency" debt that is paid in pesos (chapter 7). (Pesos.)

**ANSES.** Administración Nacional de la Seguridad Social, the national social security administration. It pays pensions and other benefits, and manages the FGS. It is part of the national public sector. (Pesos.)

**ARCA.** Agencia de Recaudación y Control Aduanero, the tax and customs agency, formerly AFIP. It collects export duties and publishes the monthly export files by tariff line.

**Dollar hoarding (atesoramiento).** Purchases of banknotes and foreign currency by individuals and firms to keep them, outside the productive system. In the BCRA's FX balance it appears as external asset formation by the non-financial private sector. (Dollars.)

**BADLAR.** Rate banks pay on fixed-term deposits of more than one million pesos, at 30 to 35 days. It serves as a reference for bonds and loans. (Pesos.)

**FX balance (balance cambiario).** The BCRA's monthly record of all purchases and sales of foreign currency in the FX market, by concept and by sector. It is the source of the table of dollar sources and uses. (Dollars.)

**Exchange-rate band (banda cambiaria).** Regime in force since April 2025: the exchange rate floats between a floor and a ceiling, and the BCRA buys or sells only at the edges.

**Cash basis (base caja).** Recording revenue or spending when the money comes in or goes out, and not when the obligation arises. Argentina's fiscal balance is published on a cash basis, and so it does not show capitalized interest until it is paid (D-009). (Pesos.)

**Monetary base (base monetaria).** Banknotes and coins in circulation plus banks' current-account deposits at the BCRA. The BCRA expands it when it buys foreign currency, lends to the Treasury or pays interest, and reduces it when it sells foreign currency or places interest-bearing liabilities. (Pesos.)

**BCB.** Banco Central do Brasil, Brazil's central bank (Annex B).

**BCRA.** Banco Central de la República Argentina, the central bank. It issues pesos, manages the reserves and operates in the FX market.

**IDB and CAF (BID y CAF).** Inter-American Development Bank and Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean. Multilateral organizations that lend to the Treasury in dollars, along with the World Bank. (Dollars.)

**Blend.** Scheme in force between December 2023 and April 2025 under which exporters sold 80% of their foreign currency in the official market and 20% through the contado con liquidación. (Dollars.)

**BONAR DUAL and BONTE.** Treasury bonds denominated in dollars and paid in pesos, at the official exchange rate or at the higher of two yields. Fiscal statistics count them as foreign currency; the report, by currency of payment, counts them in the peso circuit. (Pesos.)

**Bonares and Globales.** Treasury dollar bonds issued in the 2020 restructuring. Bonares (AL) are governed by Argentine law and Globales (GD) by New York law. (Dollars.)

**BONCAP.** Fixed-rate Treasury peso bonds that capitalize interest and pay everything at maturity. They are like LECAP, with maturities of more than one year. (Pesos.)

**BOPREAL.** Bonos para la Reconstrucción de una Argentina Libre, dollar bonds issued by the BCRA since 2024 to settle importers' commercial debts. (Dollars, issued by the BCRA.)

**CAFCI.** Cámara Argentina de Fondos Comunes de Inversión, the Argentine mutual fund association. It publishes fund assets by fund type.

**Interest capitalization (capitalización de intereses).** Interest is not paid in each period: it is added to principal and paid at maturity. The cash-basis fiscal balance does not record it until then (D-009). (Pesos.)

**Caución.** Short-term loan secured by securities, arranged on the exchange. Money market funds place part of their portfolio in cauciones. (Pesos.)

**CEPA.** Centro de Economía Política Argentina, a private research center. One of its net reserve estimates is cited.

**Cepo.** Common name for the set of restrictions on purchases of foreign currency by individuals and firms (capital controls). It was lifted for individuals on 14 April 2025.

**CER.** Coeficiente de Estabilización de Referencia. Daily index that tracks inflation with a lag of ten business days. CER bonds adjust their principal by this index. (Pesos.)

**Dollar circuit (circuito de los dólares).** The inflows and outflows of dollars to and from the country and the public sector: exports, imports, interest, international organizations, the market, dollar hoarding and reserves. It is run by exporters and lenders (D-016).

**Peso circuit (circuito de los pesos).** The inflows and outflows of pesos of the Treasury and the BCRA: taxes, spending, peso debt, money issuance and interest-bearing liabilities. It is run by the Treasury and the BCRA (D-016).

**CNV.** Comisión Nacional de Valores, the securities regulator. It regulates the capital market and publishes mutual fund portfolios.

**Consolidation (consolidación).** Netting out the debt one part of the public sector owes another (for example, the Letras Intransferibles held by the BCRA), to measure only what is owed to third parties.

**Crossing between circuits (cruce entre circuitos).** The points where public-sector pesos pass into the dollar circuit: the Treasury's purchases of dollars, from the BCRA or in the market, and dollar debt to the BCRA that the Treasury redeems with pesos (the AL35 in 2024 and the Letras Intransferibles in 2026). In the diagrams it appears as "Dollar purchases" and "Dollar debt to the BCRA redeemed with pesos" on the peso side, and as "Bought with pesos" on the dollar side (D-017).

**Cuenta de Inversión.** Annual report of the Contaduría General de la Nación (the national accounting office) on budget execution, which Congress approves or rejects. Its borrowing figures differ from those of the monthly AIF (chapter 7).

**SDR (DEG).** Special drawing right. The IMF's unit of account, made up of a basket of five currencies. Debt to the IMF is in SDRs and its dollar value changes with the basket's exchange rate.

**Export duties (derechos de exportación).** Tax the exporter pays on the FOB value of what it sells abroad. Also known as retenciones. Today they are assessed in dollars and paid in pesos.

**Third-party debt (deuda con terceros).** Public-sector debt to creditors that are not part of it: private holders, resident and non-resident, and international organizations. In pesos, the report adds the BCRA's interest-bearing liabilities to the Treasury securities in private hands, because at the end of 2023 peso absorption sat at the BCRA (chapter 2.4, D-019).

**Currency difference the source does not break down (diferencia entre monedas que la fuente no abre).** The part of the crossing between pesos and dollars that cannot be attributed to an identified operation. Over the term it was USD 4,309 million, 4% of the Treasury's dollar flow, and it is shown in the diagrams under that name instead of being allocated.

**DJVE.** Declaración jurada de venta al exterior, the sworn export sale declaration. The grain exporter registers it at the time of sale, and the duty rate on that date is the one it pays.

**Dólar linked.** Peso bonds whose principal is adjusted by the official exchange rate. They are paid in pesos, so they are not a need for dollars. (Pesos.)

**Dólar soja.** Programs in 2022 and 2023 (Programa de Incremento Exportador) that paid a higher exchange rate for soybeans sold in a short window. They brought forward FX settlements and duty payments. (Dollars.)

**Duales.** Treasury peso bonds that pay the higher of two rates: a fixed rate and a floating one (TAMAR) or a CER-adjusted one. (Pesos.)

**EFF.** Extended Fund Facility of the IMF. The April 2025 agreement is a 48-month EFF for SDR 15,267 million. (Dollars.)

**ESF.** Exchange Stabilization Fund of the US Treasury. In October 2025 it signed a swap agreement with the BCRA for up to USD 20 billion. It is distinct from the Federal Reserve, which did not take part (chapter 4.4).

**Sterilization (esterilización).** Absorbing the pesos the BCRA issues when it buys foreign currency, by placing interest-bearing liabilities (pases, LELIQ, LEFI) or, since 2025, Treasury peso debt. The interest is a cost for the issuer and income for the buyer (D-015). (Pesos.)

**FCI.** Fondo común de inversión, a mutual fund. Money market funds invest in fixed-term deposits, cauciones and sight accounts. T+1 fixed-income funds hold most of the LECAP, BONCAP and CER bonds held by funds. (Pesos.)

**FGS.** Fondo de Garantía de Sustentabilidad of ANSES, the pension reserve fund. It holds Treasury bonds, so part of the public debt is held by the public sector itself.

**IMF (FMI).** International Monetary Fund. A creditor that, in practice, is paid before the rest. (Dollars.)

**FOB.** Free on board. Value of the goods at the port of shipment, without freight or insurance. It is the base for export duties.

**FODER and guarantee bills (FODER y letras en garantía).** Trust fund for the development of renewable energy. The Treasury gives it dollar bills as a guarantee, which are paid only if a generator exercises a put option. The report treats them as contingent debt (D-011). (Dollars.)

**Hydrocarbon sliding formula (fórmula móvil de hidrocarburos).** The export duty rate on crude oil, gas and several fuels depends on the international crude price: 0% up to a base price, the cap from a reference price upward, and a straight line in between (Decreto 488/2020). Since July 2026, the cap on six fuel tariff lines falls every month until it reaches 0% in June 2027 (Decreto 566/2026). (Dollars.)

**SHA-256 hash (huella sha256).** A 64-character code computed over the content of a file, which changes if a single byte changes. The report stores each original document with its SHA-256 hash, so that anyone can verify it is the same.

**Identity between regimes (identidad entre regímenes).** Result of the Annex A model: if the BCRA buys all the dollars that are sold in the market, collecting the export duty in pesos or in dollars leaves the consolidated public sector with the same dollars and the same peso debt. What changes is the pocket (Treasury or BCRA) and the timing.

**INDEC.** Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Censos, the national statistics institute. It publishes the consumer price index (IPC) the report uses to convert each month's pesos into pesos at August 2026 prices.

**Accrued interest (interés devengado).** The interest that corresponds to a period, whether paid or not. It is distinct from interest paid (cash basis). The difference is capitalized interest (D-009). (Pesos.)

**LECAP.** Fixed-rate Treasury peso bills that capitalize interest monthly and pay everything at maturity, at less than one year. (Pesos.)

**LEFI.** Letras Fiscales de Liquidez. Treasury bills that replaced the BCRA's pases between July 2024 and July 2025. Banks held them and the Treasury paid them. (Pesos.)

**LELINK and LETES.** Treasury bills. LELINK are denominated in dollars and paid in pesos (dólar linked). Dollar LETES can be paid in dollars or, depending on the series, in pesos.

**LELIQ.** Letras de Liquidez of the BCRA, placed only with banks. They were replaced by pases in December 2023. (Pesos.)

**Letras Intransferibles.** Non-transferable dollar notes the Treasury gave the BCRA in exchange for reserves to pay debt, since 2006. They cannot be traded. Part was bought back in 2025 and 2026 with IMF funds and BCRA profits. (Dollars, debt within the public sector.)

**MEP and contado con liquidación.** Ways of buying dollars through the securities market: one buys a bond in pesos and sells it in dollars, locally (MEP) or abroad (contado con liquidación). They do not go through the official FX market and do not appear in the purchases of banknotes and foreign currency in the FX balance.

**MLC.** Mercado libre de cambios. The official market where foreign currency is bought and sold, with the BCRA as regulator and participant.

**Currency of payment (moneda de pago).** The currency in which a debt is actually paid, which may differ from its currency of denomination. A dólar linked bond is denominated in dollars and paid in pesos. The report classifies flows by currency of payment, like table A.5 of the Finance Secretariat (Secretaría de Finanzas) bulletin.

**NCM.** Nomenclatura Común del Mercosur, the Mercosur common nomenclature. The code of each product in foreign trade. Duty rates are set by NCM tariff line.

**Foreign-currency need (necesidad de divisas).** Firm debt payments settled in foreign currency in a period. The gross need includes all of them; the consolidated need nets out payments to holders within the public sector.

**NOTALIQ.** Floating-rate BCRA liquidity notes that accompanied the LELIQ until they were eliminated. (Pesos.)

**ONCP.** Oficina Nacional de Crédito Público, the national public credit office, part of the Finance Secretariat. It runs SIGADE and publishes the financial structure of each instrument.

**OPC.** Oficina de Presupuesto del Congreso, the congressional budget office. It publishes reports on debt and public credit operations with its own method.

**Pases pasivos.** Operations in which the BCRA takes pesos from banks for one or a few days and pays them interest (repos). They were the BCRA's main interest-bearing liability until July 2024. (Pesos.)

**Interest-bearing liabilities (pasivos remunerados).** The BCRA's peso debts that pay interest: pases, LELIQ and, earlier, LEBAC. In the report the LEFI and the Treasury debt placed to absorb pesos are added to them. (Pesos.)

**Repo.** Dollar loan that international banks give the BCRA against securities as collateral, which the BCRA buys back at maturity. It adds to gross reserves while outstanding.

**Gross reserves (reservas brutas).** All of the BCRA's external assets: foreign currency, gold, SDRs, IMF position.

**Net reserves (reservas netas).** Gross reserves minus the BCRA's short-term external liabilities, under the definition of the IMF agreement. The Treasury's dollar deposits at the BCRA are not subtracted (D-001).

**Joint resolution (resolución conjunta, RC).** Regulation of the Finance and Budget Secretariats that authorizes each debt issue, exchange or buyback. They are cited by number and year, as in RC 27/2026.

**Overall balance with accrued capitalized interest (resultado financiero con intereses capitalizados devengados).** The cash-basis overall balance plus the interest that LECAP, BONCAP and LEFI add to principal each quarter, even if not yet paid. It does not include the principal adjustment of CER bonds. It is the measure chapter 3.4 reconstructs (D-009).

**Primary and overall balance (resultado primario y financiero).** The primary balance is the difference between revenue and spending excluding interest. The overall balance also subtracts interest paid in cash. Neither of the two, on a cash basis, records capitalized interest (D-009). (Pesos.)

**Rollover.** Share of debt maturities renewed through new placements. A rollover of 136% means 36% more was placed than was falling due.

**Sankey (diagram).** Flow diagram in which the width of each band is proportional to the amount. In this report, inflows are on the left and outflows on the right, and each diagram is in a single currency.

**National public sector (sector público nacional, SPN).** The Central Administration (the Treasury and the ministries), decentralized agencies, ANSES with its FGS, national state-owned enterprises and trust funds. It does not include the BCRA or Banco Nación. It is the perimeter of the fiscal balance the Budget Secretariat publishes. (Pesos.)

**Selic and IPCA.** The policy rate of the Banco Central do Brasil and Brazil's consumer price index. A large part of Brazil's public debt is indexed to one or the other (Annex B).

**SIGADE.** Sistema de Gestión y Análisis de la Deuda, the debt management and analysis system. The Oficina Nacional de Crédito Público's database holding each Treasury debt operation. Its amounts are in dollars (units).

**SOFR.** Secured Overnight Financing Rate, the US dollar overnight reference rate. The BCRA's repos pay SOFR plus a spread.

**Fiscal surplus (superávit fiscal).** The State took more pesos from the private sector through taxes than it put in through spending (primary surplus) or through spending and interest (overall surplus). The pesos that return through taxes stop circulating: collecting taxes withdraws pesos from the economy, just as spending puts them in. A peso surplus is measured in pesos; to pay dollar debt with it, someone has to sell the dollars and the Treasury has to buy them. (Pesos.)

**TAMAR.** Rate large banks pay on fixed-term deposits of more than one billion pesos. It serves as a reference for dual bonds. (Pesos.)

**Effective rate (tasa efectiva).** What a nominal rate actually yields over a year when interest is reinvested. A nominal rate of 100% a year on overnight pases, reinvested every day, yields (1 + 1/365)^365 − 1 = 171% in a year. (Pesos.)

**Treasury (Tesoro).** The Tesoro Nacional, which issues Central Administration debt and collects taxes.

**Single exchange rate (tipo de cambio único).** A single price for buying and selling foreign currency in the official market, with no split rate and no blend.

**TMU.** Technical memorandum of understanding of the IMF agreement. It defines how targets are measured, among them net reserves (D-001).

**USD.** US dollars, the currency issued by the US Federal Reserve. The report expresses in USD million everything paid in foreign currency. (Dollars.)

**BCRA profits (utilidades del BCRA).** The BCRA's accounting profit transferred to the Treasury. In the Budget Secretariat's spreadsheets it appears as "rentas percibidas del BCRA" (income received from the BCRA) and sits outside revenue, so it does not improve the published fiscal balance and appears only as a source of financing. Over the term it totaled ARS 42.9 trillion at August 2026 prices, with transfers in April 2025 and May 2026. In 2024 and 2025 these profits were mostly accounting gains: revaluation of dollar assets and CER adjustment of Treasury bonds, with no cash received (chapter 3). Under article 38 of the Charter, losses are not covered by the Treasury: they are charged to the BCRA's reserves and capital. (Pesos.)

**Face value (valor nominal, VNO).** The principal written on a bond, which may differ from what is paid for it. The Treasury bought back debt from the BCRA below its face value (chapter 6).
